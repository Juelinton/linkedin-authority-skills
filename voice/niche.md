# Niche

Who you are writing for, and what you are arguing against. This is what stops the output from being
competent and generic.

Copy to `linkedin-voice/niche.md` and fill in, or run `/linkedin-authority:setup-voice`.

---

## The reader

[One person, not a segment. Their role, what their week looks like, what they are measured on, what
keeps them up. If you write "B2B decision makers" you will get posts that sound like everyone's.]

## What they already believe

[The consensus in your space. The advice they have heard a hundred times. You need this because
your job is to have a position on it, not to repeat it.]

## The enemy

[The real obstacle your content fights. Not a competitor, a force. Organizational inertia, vanity
metrics, the tooling that promises leverage and delivers admin, whatever it is for you.

This is the single most valuable line in the file. A body of content with a named enemy compounds.
One without it is a stream of tips.]

## My position

[What you believe that a competent person in your field could disagree with. If nobody could
disagree, it is not a position, it is a description.

Two or three sentences. This is the thesis your posts keep circling from different angles.]

## Vocabulary

**Terms of art, keep as-is:** [The jargon your reader uses daily and would find odd if translated
or explained. Being fluent in it is a credibility signal in itself.]

**Words I never use:** [Terms that mark an outsider, or that you find dishonest. The skills will
treat this as a hard ban.]

**If you write in a language other than English:** name which terms stay in English anyway, because
that is how your market speaks. Example for Brazilian B2B sales: champion, deal, pipeline, C-level,
discovery call and MEDDPICC stay English, while support, team and meeting get translated.

## Proof I can use publicly

[Numbers, studies, benchmarks you are allowed to cite. A post with one real number outperforms three
posts of pure opinion. Keep this list growing, it is the scarcest input.]

[Things you find interesting but that dilute your semantic signal. Naming them here lets
`/linkedin-authority:topic-score` push back on you honestly instead of writing whatever you ask for.]
